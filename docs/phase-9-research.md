## Commercial conclusion

There is no verified free universal UK commercial occupier-history API in this research. Planning, certificates, registrations, ownership and observed listings answer different questions. Combining them can supply useful dated evidence; it cannot reconstruct every tenancy or explain business failures. A specialist retail survey is the closest fit for deeper occupier/vacancy history, but buying enterprise data before validating a £29 report is not justified by the public evidence available.

Recommend existing evidence plus bounded official London planning queries, with a small reviewed-source import for actual past businesses or explicit vacancy statements. Do not build 33 council scrapers or buy an occupancy database. Keep automated coverage and operator-assisted research visibly distinct. The recommended development scope has no new fixed provider fee; it is not a claim that all existing Phase 8 providers remain free at launch.

## PropertyData cost and retention distinction

Verified monthly API tiers: 2,000 credits £28; 5,000 £48; 15,000 £96; 30,000 £144; 50,000 £192; 100,000 £288. Trial limit is 500 credits. These are subscription allowances, not cash pay-as-you-go. VAT and the actual checkout total must be confirmed before any purchase. [Published API prices](https://propertydata.co.uk/api/pricing).

At full utilisation, the smallest tier allocates £0.014 per credit. Ten planning results allocate £0.014; twenty allocate £0.028. At only ten paid reports/month, £28 is **£2.80 per report**, before Phase 8 usage, tax or unused allowance. At fifty it is £0.56; at one hundred £0.28. Do not justify a subscription using the fully utilised marginal figure alone.

The licensing summary permits enhanced customer SaaS/reports and dated historical observations, derived events and provenance. Current copies have a 60-day caching limit; historical observations must show retrieval date and never answer a present-state question. Photos are not licensed by the API. [Licensing summary](https://propertydata.co.uk/api/documentation/licensing).

The controlling terms also prohibit serving data to others without an active subscription after cancellation while allowing retained historical records and already-produced reports. Whether ongoing customer access to a particular historical report is covered should be clarified in writing before a launch relies on it. Do not assume report deletion at day 30/60 fixes every rights issue; do not refresh a ready report to evade an expiry. [Controlling terms](https://propertydata.co.uk/terms). This is a specific admission question, not a blanket rejection of the provider or a legal opinion.

## Practical pilot approach and limits

For an early paid-report pilot, a reviewed factual source can be more valuable than another large dataset. A small import of exact-premises facts from a council record or a business's own dated announcement can supply prior-business evidence without a scraper. Store the permitted factual representation and provenance, not articles/images or a searchable copy of a website. Site terms and personal-data restrictions still apply; blocked rights mean reference-only/not publishable, not automatic ingestion.

For example, Noble Rot's own [February 2020 publication](https://noblerot.co.uk/shop/magazine/issue-22-published-february-2020) identifies the Gay Hussar and announces a planned successor. Its [Soho page](https://noblerot.co.uk/restaurant/soho) supplies current venue context. This is a useful research lead for a real change-of-business benchmark, **not proof of the planned opening date, exact predecessor closure date, continuous occupation or cause of closure**. Implementation must independently verify the address/unit and actual event. Research did not import or certify this as SiteFit evidence.

Proposed operator-review cap: ten minutes for an exceptional pilot report, not every anonymous Snapshot. At an illustrative £25/hour labour assumption this is £4.17 per reviewed report; report frequency and actual time must be measured. It is a temporary bounded option, not a staffing commitment or a new admin dashboard. No search API, enterprise quote or paid document is required now.

## Findings that remain unresolved

- Live GLA endpoint/schema and explicit commercial historical-field reuse; no complete London/application-date coverage claim yet.
- PropertyData account credit balance/trial expiry/renewal status and post-cancellation serving interpretation; no permission to renew is inferred.
- Specialist API coverage, unit match, observation cadence, historical depth and durable paid-report rights: unverified until a written sample/quote exists.
- Optional council empty-property metadata retention and rights; no general London vacancy inventory.
- No measured cross-source prior-occupier recall or closure-date accuracy. This must be a small implementation pilot, not a research claim.

These do not require purchasing anything to approve the proposed architecture. Dependent sources remain unavailable until admitted; the plan specifies meaningful positive proofs and honest gap reporting rather than declaring an all-unknown timeline sufficient.
