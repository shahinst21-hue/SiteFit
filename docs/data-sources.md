## Provider acceptance checklist for later phases

For each selected dataset/endpoint, record the official documentation and verified fields; sample coverage across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland; date and geography semantics; quota/cost; failure behaviour; attribution; commercial display, storage, reuse and export permissions; and implementation owner. Provider terms may constrain the proposed snapshot/evidence schema and PDF content. Resolve restrictions before promising report content.

All providers sit behind internal adapters when implemented. Keep provenance and availability states. Use the cheapest permitted source that supports the required claim rather than collecting unneeded data. No provider listed here is assumed to supply proprietary footfall, reliable turnover, guaranteed demand, complete premises history or a business success probability.

Premises history and demand can remain Unknown or Insufficient Evidence when permitted reliable observations are unavailable. The product does not build a national historical property database or scrape property websites to hide source gaps.

## UK-wide routing decision — 2026-10-04

This register is not a live-integration claim. Future adapters must declare nation/region/local-authority coverage; choose the appropriate permitted source for the resolved geography and report explicit gaps. TfL is a regional candidate only. ONS/Census must not be treated as a uniform four-nation feed without verification. Candidate additions for later investigation include National Records of Scotland, NISRA and nation-specific statistics/planning registers, plus national/regional transport sources (including Transport Scotland, Transport for Wales and Translink). Exact endpoints, coverage, terms and suitability are unverified and must be checked before integration. No accounts, source calls or provider adapters in Phase 2.5.

Source selection is driven by decision relevance, not large datasets. Distinguish registers from measured demand, station activity from premises footfall, and business records from survival/closure causes. Geographic variation and contradictory/stale evidence must survive normalisation into AI interpretation.
## Phase 4 approved initial address provider

The explicit Phase 4 instruction supersedes the historical proposed Google address lookup: Postio is the initial provider for postcode delivery-point lookup and selected UDPRN resolution. Optional address search is genuine autocomplete only. Provider health is an explicit development probe, not a public endpoint. Primary postcode and selected-ID requests can be billable; keystrokes never call postcode lookup, no automatic paid retry is implemented and CI uses mocks.

Observed live results on 2026-10-04: KT2 7AU (England) 31 delivery points; EH1 1YZ (Scotland) 1; CF10 1EP (Wales) 4; BT1 5GS (Northern Ireland) 1. All resolved by their actual UDPRN. Country/district/ward and postcode coordinates were returned for the first three. The NI record omitted those fields: they remain null with unknown precision, without inferred geography. Counts are observations, not fixed future expectations or proof of exhaustive UK coverage.

Postio postal verification does not prove a commercial use, occupancy, exact building coordinates, footfall, demand or viability. Preserve organisation/sub-building/building/street/locality/PO Box separately where supplied; district and ward are not county. UPRN is not supplied by this integration.

Use is address capture/confirmation with selected internal canonical records. No PAF bulk cache, public inventory, provider raw-response export or marketing endorsement is created. Later commercial reuse, redistribution or report export requires a fresh licence review. Sources: [Postio address API](https://postio.co.uk/docs/address), [API errors](https://postio.co.uk/docs/errors), [PAF end-user terms](https://postio.co.uk/paf-end-user-terms) and [Postio terms](https://postio.co.uk/terms).

## Historical Phase 6 analytical adequacy proposal — superseded by D70

D66 requires interpreted decision parameters, not raw source cards. Existing residents/stops/partial food registers cannot establish present demand, competitor pressure, strong access or premises suitability. The [rewritten plan](phase6_plan.md) proposes a narrow earlier Foursquare OS Places London/category release for recorded-market comparison across coffee, restaurant and salon. No account/import/integration or source approval occurred in this review.

Current [Foursquare access documentation](https://docs.foursquare.com/data-products/docs/access-fsq-os-places) describes free data through a Places Portal/Iceberg catalogue with account/token, replacing assumptions about anonymous legacy S3 access. Its [schema](https://docs.foursquare.com/data-products/docs/places-os-data-schema) documents Apache 2.0 and date/category/coordinate semantics; refreshed is not verified trading, closed date is not verified historical closure. Exact release permissions/retention/notices and local category/coverage/precision quality need review before admission. Exclude unnecessary contact/personal fields. Free provider access does not imply zero import/hosting cost.

The reviewed [TfL PTAL resource](https://data.london.gov.uk/dataset/public-transport-accessibility-levels-24rz6) offers 2015 downloads; not adequate for a current access verdict. No WebCAT scraping is proposed. [ONS OA geography](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/geography/ukgeographies/censusgeographies/census2021geographies) is population-designed, so density background is not commercial-peer performance. Proposed analytical-readiness/comparator gates must pass before Phase 6 completion; AI cannot fill source gaps. Adequate free official sources remain preferred; paid services need explicit incremental-value/cost approval.

Standard provider abuse-monitoring retention can still apply. Provider-specific permitted normalised/derived/reference/timestamp representation and original retrieval/licence metadata are preserved; raw responses are not blanket permanent report content.
